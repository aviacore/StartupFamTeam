# Researcher role playbook

Use this playbook to turn a broad team need into a concrete Researcher request.

## CTO / Fullstack / Developer

Good requests:

- "What is the current Node.js LTS line, and is it safe for our stack?"
- "Compare Prisma and Drizzle for a small Next.js SaaS MVP."
- "What changed in React 19 that affects app architecture or dependencies?"
- "Which free hosting tier is best for a Next.js prototype?"

Expected output:

- recommendation first;
- official docs or changelogs linked;
- compatibility and migration risks;
- constraints that affect implementation speed.

## PM / CEO

Good requests:

- "How do users currently solve problem X without our product?"
- "List existing alternatives to service Y and their positioning."
- "What evidence exists for demand around keyword or workflow Z?"

Expected output:

- user behavior summary;
- competitor or alternative table;
- available demand signals and their limits;
- decision implication for scope or positioning.

## Marketer

Good requests:

- "Who are the main competitors and what channels do they use?"
- "Which communities discuss this topic?"
- "What messages appear repeatedly in customer-facing content?"

Expected output:

- competitor/channel list;
- community links and audience notes;
- repeated pain points, objections, and wording;
- content or campaign angle suggestions grounded in evidence.

## Business Analyst

Good requests:

- "Which business models are common in this niche?"
- "What are visible pricing ranges for similar tools?"
- "What public data exists for ad costs or acquisition channels?"

Expected output:

- pricing and monetization patterns;
- unit-economics assumptions with source quality noted;
- region, currency, and date checked;
- uncertainty and follow-up data needs.

## Escalation rule

If a request needs paid data, account access, customer interviews, or a board
decision, the Researcher should state that clearly and return the best available
public evidence instead of inventing a number.
