# Researcher source checklist

Use this checklist before sending a Researcher answer. It helps keep findings
factual, sourced, and honest about uncertainty.

## Source quality ladder

Prefer higher tiers when they are available.

1. Primary source
   - official docs, changelogs, release notes;
   - vendor pricing pages;
   - company websites and help centers;
   - standards bodies, regulators, app stores, public datasets.
2. Reputable secondary source
   - established technical publications;
   - analyst reports or benchmark writeups with visible methodology;
   - interviews, conference talks, or podcasts from accountable people.
3. Community signal
   - GitHub issues, Reddit, Hacker News, Discord, Telegram, X/Twitter;
   - useful for pain points, sentiment, and language users actually use;
   - not enough by itself for market size, pricing, or technical claims.
4. Weak signal
   - unsourced blog posts, SEO listicles, scraped directories;
   - use only to discover leads, then verify elsewhere.

## Freshness rules

Check the publication or update date for:

- software versions and LTS status;
- pricing and free-tier limits;
- hosting availability and quotas;
- platform policies;
- competitor positioning;
- advertising costs and channel rules.

If no date is visible, say that freshness could not be verified.

## Claim types and evidence

| Claim type | Minimum evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Current version / feature | Official docs, changelog, or release notes |
| Pricing / plan limit | Vendor pricing page or official terms |
| Competitor exists | Company site or product listing |
| Competitor positioning | Landing page copy, docs, or pricing page |
| User pain point | Multiple community examples or customer reviews |
| Market size / search demand | Named dataset or tool with date and geography |
| Performance benchmark | Methodology, environment, and reproducible setup |
| Legal / compliance | Regulator, official policy, or qualified legal source |

## Red flags

Do not present a claim as fact when:

- the only source is an AI-generated answer;
- the page has no date and the topic changes frequently;
- pricing is copied from a third-party listicle;
- a benchmark does not describe setup or dataset;
- a community comment is treated as representative market data;
- numbers are rounded without explaining the source or method.

## Confidence labels

Use these labels in the final answer:

- High: primary sources agree and the fact is current.
- Medium: credible sources agree, but data is incomplete or indirect.
- Low: evidence is sparse, old, geographically unclear, or mostly anecdotal.

When confidence is medium or low, include the best next verification step.

## Final self-check

Before sending:

- Are the most important facts sourced?
- Are dates included for current facts?
- Are assumptions stated?
- Are gaps named instead of hidden?
- Is the answer short enough for the requester to act on?
